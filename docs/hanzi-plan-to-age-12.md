# Hanzi learning and app content plan through age 12

Prepared 25 September 2026. Scope: a six-year progression from approximately age 6 to age 12; simplified characters and Hanyu Pinyin; a child who speaks Mandarin comfortably and is beginning literacy; parent-directed home learning on an iPad.

**Prepare a library of 3,500 distinct simplified characters: 3,000 core reading targets and 500 optional extension targets. Within the core, designate up to 2,500 as handwriting targets for a full primary-school pathway.** Writing targets overlap the reading set. Multiple pronunciations, example words and review attempts do not add to the distinct-character count. The current prototype contains six characters; this document defines its expansion rather than claiming that the full content already exists.

## 1. The benchmark and its limits

The inspected 2022 Mainland curriculum gives these approximate cumulative stage-end counts. Age bands are an illustrative mapping to six years of primary school; the source specifies grades.

| Reference stage | Approximate age band | Recognise/read | Write | Proposed teaching emphasis |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Grades 1–2 | 6–8 | 1,600 | 800 | Familiar spoken words, pinyin, basic strokes and common components; short supported reading |
| Grades 3–4 | 8–10 | 2,500 | 1,600 | Word families, useful component clues, similar-looking characters, sentences and short passages |
| Grades 5–6 | 10–12 | 3,000 | 2,500 | Context-dependent readings, independent reading, dictation in words and purposeful written paragraphs |

Count source: Ministry of Education, *义务教育语文课程标准（2022年版）*, printed pp. 7, 9 and 11 (PDF pp. 14, 16 and 18), visually checked in the [source PDF](https://www.hnwu.edu.cn/ewebeditor/uploadfile/20231006195355939.pdf), also available as a [university-hosted scan](https://www.hnwu.edu.cn/ewebeditor/uploadfile/20231006195355939.pdf). The teaching-emphasis column is this plan’s synthesis.

These are schooling benchmarks. They are useful for sizing the app and offering a demanding pathway; a home learner’s pace should follow retained learning and available reading/practice time. Reading competence also needs vocabulary, comprehension and connected text.

A 2025 revision exists: the [Higher Education Press catalogue](https://xuanshu.hep.com.cn/front/h5Mobile/bookDetails?bookId=697a4250e119ac972922bcc9) identifies the revised standard and its 2026 official interpretation. The revised full text has not yet been inspected here. Consequently, the table is explicitly the verified **2022 benchmark**, and this plan makes no claim of complete alignment with the revised standard. Check the chosen school’s current list before assigning grade labels to individual characters.

## 2. Which characters to prepare

Use the curriculum’s 300-character basic list and its 3,500-character common-character inventory as starting references. The latter is split into lists of 2,500 and 1,000. These were verified at printed pp. 66 and 70 (PDF pp. 73 and 77). The 3,500 inventory serves compulsory education as a whole; its existence does not make all 3,500 an age-12 requirement.

Select and sequence the app’s 3,000 core targets using the child’s school list when available, familiar useful words, occurrence in the reading material, reusable components and gradual complexity. Choose the remaining 500 as extension content. The 3,000/500 split is our product design, not an official prescribed split. The 300-character basic list is a starting reference, not a fixed teaching order; familiar functional words and the child’s name may deserve earlier attention.

Maintain one reviewed record per character, with:

- Its simplified form, stable identifier, source, content version and intended level.
- Word-linked readings and tone-marked pinyin, including separate entries where pronunciation changes with meaning/context; for example, 行 in 行走 and 银行.
- A child-friendly explanation, two useful example-word associations and at least one original short sentence.
- Checked character/word audio and licensed stroke data, including variant and stroke-model review.
- Separate reading and writing eligibility, likely confusions, hints and several assessment prompts.

Two example-word associations per character imply 7,000 associations at full library size; the number of distinct words will be lower because words may be reused. Audio quantities depend on the number of reviewed readings and examples, so do not budget a single audio file as complete coverage of every character.

## 3. Release the content progressively

Each row is the cumulative library size after a release. These are editorial milestones, not automatic learner deadlines.

| Release | Total characters available | Newly prepared | Acceptance focus |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Current prototype | 6 | 6 | Complete practice/save/recovery flow |
| Pilot course | 100 | 94 | Fully reviewed teaching sequence, pinyin, audio, exercises and short reading |
| Starter course | 300 | 200 | Home usability, delayed recall, handwriting transfer and useful component families |
| Growing library | 800 | 500 | More words and connected text; parent pacing and review across multiple units |
| Early-primary coverage | 1,600 | 800 | Separate writing selection; school-list mapping and contextual assessment |
| Middle-primary coverage | 2,500 | 900 | Longer reading, common confusions and word-dependent pronunciation |
| Full primary core | 3,000 | 500 | Broader reading and useful writing, with continued review of earlier items |
| Optional extension | 3,500 | 500 | Child interests and preparation for further reading |

Budget the whole library, but finish and trial the first 100 before mass-producing thousands of lessons. Retain a reusable lesson/assessment template; teach characters within meaningful words and reading material. A production package may contain 50 characters, while an individual child session still introduces only a few.

## 4. Home learning from age 6 onward

**At the beginning:** place the child using a short, encouraging sample of plain characters, familiar words, pinyin and a few paper-writing tasks. Start in the first 100-character course, skipping known items. Begin around five new recognition targets and two or three selected writing targets per week, using the existing [15–20-minute routine](teaching-plan.md). Add shared reading, with an adult supplying untaught text. These are adjustable starting choices.

**As reading becomes established:** move through the 300- and 800-character libraries; connect recurring parts and word families, add short sentences and let the child use pinyin or audio to explore unfamiliar material. Keep pronunciation help available during learning, and record whether it was used during checks. Introduce more independent paper writing when the child is ready.

**Through the later primary years:** extend toward the 1,600-, 2,500- and 3,000-character libraries as evidence supports it. Spend increasing time reading stories/information, explaining meaning and writing useful sentences or short paragraphs. Maintain pinyin for decoding, dictionary lookup and context-dependent readings; the app should eventually accept valid combinations beyond the prototype’s restricted letter keys.

Age guides the topics and presentation. Placement, demonstrated skill and parent choice determine lesson difficulty. A ten-year-old beginner needs age-appropriate interests alongside beginner literacy content.

The workload must match the goal. Assuming six years and 35–40 learning weeks per year:

| Scenario | Arithmetic across six years |
|---|---|
| Five new recognition targets each week | 1,050–1,200 distinct targets introduced |
| Two to three new writing targets each week | 420–720 distinct targets introduced |
| Introduce 3,000 reading targets | Average 12.5–14.3 new targets per learning week |
| Introduce 2,500 writing targets | Average 10.4–11.9 new targets per learning week |

These calculations assume every new target is distinct. They exclude time for relearning and do not forecast retained mastery. A sustained low-dose home path will cover less than the full library by age 12; reaching the full school-level target requires a substantial increase in later practice and reading. Set a term-sized goal from observed progress rather than assigning a six-year daily quota.

## 5. Feedback, checks and progress

Use separate records for character reading, meaning in context, pinyin reading/spelling, supported screen writing, independent screen writing and parent-checked paper writing. Finger tracing alone cannot establish conventional handwriting.

During teaching, give immediate, specific help. During a short assessment, record the first response and help used, then review answers together afterwards. Check different examples from the exact teaching cards so a memorised layout does not count as reading transfer.

Suggested adjustable defaults:

- Revisit items after a delay and in mixed order. A few successful later-day recalls across different contexts are stronger evidence than completing one session.
- Sample five to ten older reading items and two or three writing items in a weekly check, keeping it untimed. A parent can record oral responses and meaningful-word dictation; automatic speech grading is not required.
- Increase new items only when the child remains comfortable and delayed retention is good. The earlier plan’s roughly four-out-of-five sample is a practical signal, not a validated mastery threshold; avoid large pace changes from one tiny sample.
- Let parents pause new teaching, choose school words, select a smaller writing subset and change the amount of review. No age-locked progression or single combined “Chinese score”.

Report introduced, independently recalled, helped and due-for-review counts separately for each skill. Keep a dated evidence trail and exportable backups. Treat every “known” count as an estimate based on sampled performance.

## 6. App work needed for this scale

Replace the prototype’s embedded six-character list with a validated, versioned content catalogue and unit manifests. Keep content selection separate from each child’s history. Expand pinyin input, generate exams from the parent-enabled taught set, and serve audio/stroke data on demand. Preserve current family records and stable character identifiers during migration.

Before publishing each content release, verify unique counts, simplified forms, readings in example words, audio, answer keys, stroke-data availability and licences, and sentence difficulty. Test the lesson on the actual iPad, including finger accuracy, rotation, interruption and resumption. Missing stroke data should remain explicitly unavailable until reviewed rather than being silently replaced with a different character form.
